China, Navalmanack, and Automated Investing | Newsletter #2

Jimmy Cerone

24 Dec 2021 — 2 min read
Share

I read lots of articles these days (and other content) and as part of my morning pages, I write about them. In the spirit of selling my by-products, I’m compiling my favorite ten things from the week to further pollute your backlog.

  1. The Triumph and Terror of Wang Huning — A stunning shallow dive into the recent history of China.
  2. Your Ride Wit Us Now by Propaganda — A fascinating podcast about gerrymandering.
  3. The Almanack of Naval Ravikant — A wide ranging (and free to download) book about wealth and tech.
  4. Web3 University — My favorite guide to the block chain so far. A nice gentle introduction with links to more complex topics if you so wish.
  5. Composer — A fascinating app to bring hedge fund automation tools to the average investor.
  6. The Gulag Archipelago by Aleksandr Isaevich Solzhenitsyn — A terrifying peek behind the old iron curtain.
  7. Happy, Smart and Useful by Derek Sivers — A short article with an interesting frame for decision making.
  8. What is the Business Model of DuckDuckGo? — A damning article that shows that Google doesn’t need to be evil to make money.
  9. How This All Happened by Morgan Housel — A short but illuminating look at the history of the US economy since 1950’s.
  10. a new chapter: full-time working from a van in a forest — Part memoir part tech article, a fascinating story about a software developer who hit a rough patch and developed from a van.

May 2026 Post Script

I first wrote this post almost 5 years ago. While that feels like light years ago, I am tickled by how many of these articles remain relevant. Web3 (though no one calls it that anymore) is finding real utility with a fellow buzzword (AI). Gerrymandering is becoming an all out war as we approach the 2026 midterms.

Most interesting to me now though is the article about Wang Huning. I am rereading it now as I'm reading Breakneck by Dan Wang. Wang Huning feels like the hidden piece of the China puzzle, the causal link between the era of Mao and Xi. While his influence on Chinese politics is as inscrutable as it is undeniable, it is his thoughts on America which make me ponder.

Ultimately, he argues, when faced with critical social issues like drug addiction, America’s atomized, deracinated, and dispirited society has found itself with “an insurmountable problem” because it no longer has any coherent conceptual grounds from which to mount any resistance.

It is difficult to read such a clear eyed yet harsh diagnosis of the problems of America. While Breakneck focuses on the technical shortcomings of America, Huning gets at the soul of America. We are clearly in need of a revitalization here. I am far from certain we can pull it off. The past gives me hope, even so.

America is full of contradictions, mistakes and tragedies. Our government and our people are powerful and we've used our power both well and poorly. I'm hopeful that we can learn from the perspectives of those from without to help us renew ourselves from within.
