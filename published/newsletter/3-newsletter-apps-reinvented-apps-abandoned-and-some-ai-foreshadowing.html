Apps Reinvented, Apps Abandoned and Some AI Foreshadowing | Newsletter #3

Jimmy Cerone

31 Dec 2021 — 3 min read
Share

I read lots of articles these days (and other content) and as part of my morning pages, I write about them. In the spirit of selling my by-products, I’m compiling my favorite ten things from the week to further pollute your backlog.

  1. Chefling — A project management tool for your kitchen to help you eat healthier AND use all your food in the new year.
I am blown away by the fact this is about to pivot into an AI powered camera for your fridge. What a strange world we live in. Maybe that's useful combined with AI agents to automatically order food?
  1. Timestripe — My favorite way to visualize long term goals, hands down. I sometimes just open this app to ogle at the UI.
This one did not age well. I love the idea of the app, but someone gave up on this. Might be worth getting Claude to take a stab at rebuilding it?
  1. Matter — This has miraculously overtaken my Remarkable as my favorite way to read articles. A stunning upset.
Oh how the times change. While I still admire the aesthetic of Matter, I'm a Readwise loyalist for their API and general ease of integration with everything.
  1. Five Decades of Access to Tools — The first article in a series about our misguided obsession with tools has me on the edge of my seat waiting for the next installment.
Tragically this link fell victim to link rot. Happily, I found it on the Internet Archive! Interesting in light of the Pope's latest encyclical, who I believe said it better... Tools are not neutral, though they can be used for good or evil. The intentions and context of their creators matter.
  1. A Chemical Hunger — I think this easily my favorite article of all time. Another series that reads like a TV show.
I need to reread this series, it remains so fond in my memory. Whether you believe in the conclusions or not, this is science writing at its best. Thrilling, well researched and thought provoking. Writing on the web at its best.
  1. How we bootstrapped our open source Google Analytics alternative to $500k ARR — A fascinating behind the scenes look at a company just blasting off.
I'm not sure if we will see more or less of these stories in the age of AI. Contrary to the thinking of most, coding was never the bottleneck. Distribution was.
  1. Programming is Forgetting: Toward a New Hacker Ethic — One of the first critiques of hacker culture I’ve ever read. Even handed yet bold.
Yet another one I need to reread / watch. I feel this re-investigation of tech history is ever more critical as "hackers" move from the fringes to the center of power.
  1. Personal AI — I’m truly unsure of what to make of this one. A service that takes in everything you do and helps you sift through it with AI. Not sure if it’s creepy or revolutionary.
I'm struggling to wrap my head around what this became... As far as I can tell it's agentic AI with memory built on top of phone lines? A little crazy, but the distribution is there. This is one to watch.
  1. How to Take Smart Notes — My favorite introduction to the Zettelkasten method (and I’ve read a lot of them).
Ah the days I took notes. I would love to get back to this habit, but I've largely given up the pursuit of recording everything I've ever read and learned. I would say I'm better for it, but I do miss the clarity of thinking my insanity occasionally wrought.
  1. What if Git Worked With Programming Languages — An article ostensibly about a programming tool but that has far reaching consequences for how we use and understand tools in general.
I am a little giddy at reading this. Git but aware of programming language syntax? My nerd is showing... In the age of agents, what this author is proposing could be a step change in code quality.
